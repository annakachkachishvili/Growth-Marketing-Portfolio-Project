# Methodology — how I chose and vetted these experts

The brief's first evaluation criterion is *"did you find genuinely strong voices, or just the first Google results?"* This document explains how I made sure it was the former.

## Where I looked (not listicles)

I deliberately avoided "Top 10 SEO experts" articles — those are SEO-optimised to rank, not curated for quality. Instead I sourced candidates from places where practitioners actually are:

- **Conference speaker lists** (BrightonSEO, SEO Week / iPullRank) — already vetted for credibility.
- **LinkedIn** — searching `AI SEO`, `programmatic SEO`, `GEO`, `content engineering`, and following who strong people cite and argue with.
- **YouTube** — sorted by recent uploads to find who is actually active.
- **Newsletters & podcasts** — SEOFOMO, Growth Memo, Authority Hacker, Crawling Mondays.
- **Tracing citations** — once I had a few strong voices, I followed who *they* reference. Experts cluster.

## The 6 criteria each candidate had to pass

I scored a longer candidate list against these, then kept the strongest 10:

| # | Criterion | What I looked for |
|---|-----------|-------------------|
| 1 | **Practitioner proof** | Shows their *own* results — dashboards, case studies, live experiments, a tool/agency they run. |
| 2 | **Recency** | Published real content in the last ~1–3 months. |
| 3 | **Signal / specificity** | Tactical and reproducible, not generic "10 tips." |
| 4 | **Original thinking** | A point of view; willing to disagree with consensus. |
| 5 | **Collectability** | Active, official LinkedIn or YouTube presence I can actually collect from. |
| 6 | **Relevance** | Specifically about AI-powered SEO *content production*, ideally B2B SaaS. |

## Deliberate balance, not ten of the same voice

The 10 are chosen to span the whole production pipeline so the material can support a real playbook:

- **Production at scale:** Jake Ward, Jesse Cunningham, Julian Goldie
- **Content process & optimization:** Ryan Law, Koray Gübür
- **AI-search strategy & measurement:** Kevin Indig, Steve Toth, Aleyda Solís
- **Workflows + a thoughtful skeptic of mass AI content:** Gael Breton
- **Quality & judgment ("good vs. AI garbage"):** Lily Ray

Including a skeptic (Breton) and a quality hawk (Ray) is intentional — it keeps the set honest about *when AI content fails*, which is exactly the judgment this role is testing.

## The verification step (where most of the work was)

Reputation isn't enough — I checked each person's **current activity** before finalising, and it changed the list:

- I confirmed each YouTube channel's live uploads (June 2026). Only **Jesse Cunningham** and **Julian Goldie** were both recent *and* on-topic. Others (e.g. one channel's newest video was 9 months old; another had drifted to AI-industry news) were collected from LinkedIn instead, where they remain active.
- For the strategy/measurement slot I selected **Kevin Indig**, whose *current* LinkedIn work on AI-search measurement is a sharper, more relevant fit than some bigger-but-less-on-topic names.

The principle: the 10 should reflect who is producing the strongest, most relevant material **right now**, not who looked best on a list.

## Who I considered but cut (and why)

- **Mike King (iPullRank)** — elite, but his signature output is dense conference talks/blog posts (hard to collect) and leans technical GEO over content production. Top alternate.
- **Eli Schwartz** — book + Substack + podcast *guest* (not host); harder to collect, more strategy than production.
- **Nathan Gotch** — couldn't verify recent on-topic activity; lowest signal.
