What is an Authority Signature?

Google has a concept called "Authority Signature," which appears in a patent that carries "Topic Authority" in its name.

The patent "Determining Topic Authority" explains that an author's expertise, and the author's signature on a document associated with a specific topic, can increase the topical authority of that document.

In other words, Google can evaluate not only the document and the website, but also the person-like entity behind the content.

This becomes even more interesting today because Google is reintroducing famous "authors," "people," and "answers" in a social-like format through Google Profiles.

According to the patent, the simplified formula for Authority Signature is:

Authority Signature Update = Authorship Percentage of Author A for Topic T in Document D × Topical Weight of Topic T in Document D

For example, imagine a document covering two topics — Topic A: 70% of the document, Topic B: 30%. Three authors contributed. For Topic A: User X wrote 80%, User Y wrote 20%. For Topic B: User Z wrote 100%.

The authority contribution is calculated like this:
- User X on Topic A: 0.80 × 0.70 = 0.56
- User Y on Topic A: 0.20 × 0.70 = 0.14
- User Z on Topic B: 1.00 × 0.30 = 0.30

This means the system does not only evaluate whether an author exists. It can also evaluate: which topic the author contributes to, how much of the document belongs to that topic, how much of that topical section the author actually authored, and how consistently the author contributes to the same topic across different documents.

Topical Authority is not only about how much a website covers a topic. It is also about who covers the topic, how consistently they cover it, and whether the system can recognize the author as a real entity with a measurable topical signature.

Ranking #1 in the UK for "hair transplant Turkey."

This page was designed with Visual Semantics and written according to Algorithmic Authorship rules.

When we create a page, we technically follow four steps:

1. Visualization
2. Verbalization
3. Commercialization
4. Contextualization

For the hair transplant industry, the most important attributes are clear: before-after results, cost, procedure steps, hair transplant types, customer reviews, doctor and expert validation.

These attributes should not exist separately. They should be connected, multiplied, and structured across the entire page experience.

Because a page doesn't rank only because it has text. It ranks because its visual, verbal, commercial, and contextual signals support the same search intent from multiple angles.

There was also an entitization problem with this brand. Its authority was diluted across three different domains. I used the Knowledge Graph API to understand whether these domains were sharing ranking signals for the same brand. Then, we moved the two extra domains into the main domain to consolidate the ranking signals. After consolidation, the same documents started to perform better.

A luxury travel brand's last 6 months of SEO results:

- Total clicks: +46.7% (24K vs 16.4K)
- Total impressions: +123.7% (5.26M vs 2.35M)
- Average CTR: -28.6% (0.5% vs 0.7%)
- Average position: +58.2% improvement (9.4 vs 22.5)

The company's main obstacle is competing against highly authoritative provider brands, while large aggregators leverage forum and UGC content with limited visual semantics. In this type of market, the website still has to rank for commercial terms. Thus, we heavily focused on the informational segment of the topical map and used it to support the commercial sections through contextual internal linking.

During the project, no backlink-related progress was implemented. According to Microsoft Bing data, the website received over 500,000 impressions from AI search features, along with 28,000 citations.

If the central entity is "safari," you need to cover all possible attributes and relational contexts around it, such as: safari + vaccines, + luggage, + lodge types, + routes, + seasons, + safety, + itineraries, + local regions, + travel requirements. All possible predicates and relations around the context should be organized as a connected content network.

Good news for SEOs: Google is bringing Generative AI Performance reporting to Google Search Console.

At first, it will mainly help us track AI appearances and impressions, but this is already a meaningful step forward. Because we can finally start separating traditional web-result impressions from AI-result impressions more clearly.

For Holistic SEOs, this opens many new analytical layers:
- Compare crawl stats and specific user agents with AI appearances.
- Compare sentence structures, content freshness, and source responsiveness against AI visibility.
- Compare AI performance with the average position of commercial queries.
- Compare free-tool query segments with AI appearances to understand whether helpful utility-based assets support broader brand and topical relevance.

AI visibility is not only about being mentioned. It is about understanding why a source is selected, when it is selected, and which semantic, technical, and behavioral signals support that selection.
