The worst thing you can do for your AI search visibility is destroy your SEO.

I've shared dozens of examples throughout the year of sites quickly losing SEO visibility on Google, with ChatGPT citations following the same trajectory shortly after. ("If you fall in Google, you fall in AI.")

Yet many of the things I've seen folks recommending for GEO are bad ideas for SEO.

I believe a lot of this stems from new GEO practitioners not having enough SEO experience to make good long-term decisions. SEO isn't something you can learn overnight. You need years of experience to understand the cyclical nature of how once-effective tactics can actually become liabilities once search engines start identifying and treating them as spam.

This is especially true for the "too good to be true" tactics that get popularized and shared throughout the industry. If you're comfortable with rapid growth for a few months and then crashing on the other side, go for it. But most brands can't take that kind of risk.

Years of SEO experience give you the context to anticipate when a too-good-to-be-true tactic is really just a loophole, and to play "defensive" SEO. SEO is as much about what NOT to do as it is about what to do.

We've been in a bit of a gold rush with AI search for the past two years, where lots of new approaches have truly worked to manipulate AI responses and citations for a short period. But those tactics are clearly catching up to many sites now. Plus, we now have Google employees publicly discouraging "inauthentic mentions" and other GEO hacks, so we know it's on their radar.

In my opinion, the best approach is the one that leaves your brand being cited and recommended *after* the AI companies clean up the spam.

Here's a little hack I've been using to quickly convert SEO keywords into relevant AI prompts at scale directly in Google sheets.

Use the =AI or =Gemini function — plus this prompt — to convert your SEO keywords into associated ways that users might type (or ask out loud) that same question into an AI assistant.

It's not perfect, but what I like about it is that it allows you to take keywords with real search volume (import from Ahrefs, Semrush, Similarweb, etc.) and quickly convert them into AI prompts at scale.

Prompt:

`=AI("You are converting a terse SEO search keyword into the natural-language query a real person would actually type or speak to an AI assistant like ChatGPT, Gemini, or a voice assistant. Rewrite the keyword as a single conversational question or request that preserves the exact search intent and intent type (informational, commercial, transactional, or local). Phrase it the way someone would genuinely ask out loud in a full sentence — not in keyword shorthand. Keep any brand, product, or location named in the keyword, but do NOT invent specifics, constraints, or details that aren't already implied. Do not answer the query. Return only the rewritten prompt as plain text — no quotation marks, no preamble, no explanation, no trailing punctuation beyond a question mark. Keyword:", A2)`
