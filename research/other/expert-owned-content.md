# Expert owned content — blogs, newsletters & deep guides

The LinkedIn posts and YouTube transcripts in this repo are the experts' *short-form* output. Their **long-form** work — blog posts, newsletters, free courses, and in-depth guides on their own sites — is usually deeper and more structured, and it's the best raw material for a real playbook.

This is a curated, annotated index of each expert's owned channels and their strongest AI-SEO-content pieces (verified June 2026). Each entry notes *why it matters for the playbook.*

> These are pointers to the experts' own published work — links + annotations, not copies of their content.

## 1. Jake Ward
- **Owned channels:** [jakeward.io](https://jakeward.io/) (weekly growth newsletter) · **Byword** ([byword.ai](https://byword.ai)) — his AI long-form content tool.
- **Worth reading:** his "SEO heist" / programmatic-SEO case studies (1,800+ AI articles → millions of visits) and "how I create SEO content plans" breakdowns.

## 3. Kevin Indig
- **Owned channel:** [Growth Memo](https://www.growth-memo.com/) (newsletter) · [kevin-indig.com](https://www.kevin-indig.com/).
- **Flagship pieces:**
  - [The science of how AI picks its sources](https://www.growth-memo.com/p/the-science-of-how-ai-picks-its-sources) — an analysis of 1.2M AI answers and 18,012 citations found a "ski ramp" pattern: **~44% of citations come from the first 30% of a page's content.** Implication: front-load key insights; structured, briefing-style content beats narrative "ultimate guides" for AI retrieval.
  - [The ghost citation problem](https://www.growth-memo.com/p/the-ghost-citation-problem) — when AI cites a source that doesn't actually support the claim.
- **Why it matters:** the most rigorous, data-backed answers to "what actually gets you cited" — directly shapes how content should be structured.
