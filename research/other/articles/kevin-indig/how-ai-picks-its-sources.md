# The science of how AI picks its sources

- **Author:** Kevin Indig (Growth advisor; Growth Memo)
- **Source:** https://www.growth-memo.com/p/the-science-of-how-ai-picks-its-sources (covered by [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/chatgpt-citations-content-study-469483))
- **Published:** Feb 2026
- **Captured:** 2026-06-12 — *notes drawn from the article and Search Engine Land's coverage of the study.*

## Core finding
Across **1.2 million AI answers and 18,012 verified citations**, citations follow a "ski ramp" pattern: **~44% of citations come from the first 30% of a page's content.** The pattern held across randomized validation batches.

## Why this happens
LLMs are trained heavily on journalism and academic writing that use a **"bottom line up front"** structure, so they over-weight the opening of a document. ChatGPT also favours passages with **direct definitions, a balanced (non-hype) tone, and dense entities.**

## What to do about it
- **Front-load the key insight** at both article and paragraph level — don't bury the answer under a long narrative intro.
- Prioritize **clarity and information density** over clever first sentences.
- **Structured, briefing-style content outperforms narrative "ultimate guides"** for AI retrieval — a real break from a decade of long-form-narrative advice.

## How it connects to the rest of the research
- Reinforces **Steve Toth's** "declarative passages / short dependency hops" and the **"chunking"** advice in Aleyda's roadmap.
- Complements **Aleyda Solís** (she's about *how to measure* visibility; this is about *what gets cited*).

## Playbook takeaway
Content structure is now a citation lever. The writing standard should require **answer-first openings, defined terms, entity-dense early sections, and a measured tone** — then verify with prompt tracking.
