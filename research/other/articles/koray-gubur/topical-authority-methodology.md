## Core concept
**Topical Authority = Topical Coverage + Historical Data.** To rank (and be cited), a site must cover *all* the semantic variations, sub-topics and entailed queries of a niche (coverage) while building a track record of reliability and trust (historical data). Goal: be processed as *the* comprehensive expert source for a whole topic — not a single keyword.

## Key building blocks
- **Topical map** — a structured plan of every sub-topic, entity and query variation, organized into a **semantic content network** (pillar/core nodes + supporting nodes) connected by contextual internal links.
- **Entities & semantics over keywords** — build around entities, their attributes and relations (predicates), aligning with how Google's NLP and LLMs interpret meaning. (His "Authority Signature" post: Google can attribute topical authority to *authors* per-topic.)
- **Cost of Retrieval** — make it cheap/efficient for an engine to crawl, understand and rank your content (clean structure, fast delivery, unambiguous semantics). Lower retrieval cost = better treatment.
- **Momentum & history** — publish the network with coverage and consistency so the site accrues topical trust over time.

## From his recent practice (his posts in this repo)
- **Visual Semantics + Algorithmic Authorship** — a page should support one intent from multiple angles (visual, verbal, commercial, contextual), not just text.
- **Entitization fixes** — consolidating authority diluted across multiple domains into one (diagnosed via the Knowledge Graph API), after which the same documents performed better.
- Applied to large **programmatic SEO** page groups, where small per-template relevance gains multiply.

## Playbook takeaway
Before producing at scale, build the **topical map**: define the entities, sub-topics and query network for the category, then structure content as a connected semantic network with disciplined internal linking and a clean, low-retrieval-cost technical setup. Coverage + structure + history is what compounds — the durable opposite of disconnected, keyword-by-keyword AI articles.
