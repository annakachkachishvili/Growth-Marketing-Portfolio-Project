# Key studies, data & primary sources

While collecting the experts' posts, several of them repeatedly pointed to the same underlying research, data studies, and primary documents. I've gathered those here, because the *evidence behind* the experts' claims is exactly what a real playbook needs to cite. Each entry notes where I encountered it and why it matters.

> Note on links: where I'm confident of the canonical source I've linked it; where I only saw it shared via a LinkedIn post, I've named the author/publisher so it can be found directly rather than guessing a URL.

## AI-search visibility data

**Ahrefs — AI Search benchmark report (100M+ data points, 13 studies)**
Source: Ahrefs blog/research, surfaced via **Ryan Law** & Glen Allsopp.
Key findings cited: across 75K brands, **YouTube mentions correlated most strongly with AI visibility**; AI Overviews appear for ~21% of keywords; Google still sends ~190× more traffic than ChatGPT.
→ Why it matters: hard data on what actually drives AI citations (and a reality check on AI-vs-Google traffic scale).

**Tomek Rudzki / Peec AI — analysis of 500,000 prompts**
Surfaced via **Lily Ray**.
Key finding: **AI Overviews appeared in ~87% of prompts** (88.5% of commercial, decision-stage prompts), yet they're under-discussed vs ChatGPT/Perplexity.
→ Why it matters: argues AI Overviews are the largest, most commercially relevant AI surface — don't optimise only for chatbots.

**Omniscient Digital — analysis of 23,000+ AI citations**
Surfaced via **Steve Toth**.
Key finding: for branded queries, **57% of citations went to third-party content** (reviews, listicles, forums, case studies) — not owned pages.
→ Why it matters: shows where to invest (third-party consensus) and why a generic prompt tracker can mislead.

**Rand Fishkin / Similarweb — zero-click search (2026)**
Surfaced via **Aleyda Solís**.
Key finding: **fewer than one-third of Google searches still send a click.** Recommendation: replace "traffic" as a KPI; invest where your audience actually pays attention.
→ Why it matters: the strategic backdrop for the whole topic — the click economy is shrinking.

## Primary documents (Google)

**Google patent — "Determining Topic Authority" (the "Authority Signature")**
Searchable on Google Patents; surfaced via **Koray Gübür**.
Idea: Google can attribute topical authority to *authors* per-topic (Authorship % × Topical Weight), not just to pages/sites.
→ Why it matters: supports an author/entity-led content strategy, not just page-level optimisation.

**Google — "people-first / helpful content" self-assessment (32 questions)**
developers.google.com/search (Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content); surfaced via **Steve Toth** (who built a custom GPT to grade content against it).
→ Why it matters: a concrete, Google-published QA rubric for content quality.

**Google Search Console — new Generative AI performance reports**
Google Search Central blog; surfaced via **Koray Gübür, Steve Toth, Lily Ray**.
Detail: reports show AI-feature impressions/pages/countries/devices — **but no queries and no clicks.**
→ Why it matters: the first official AI-visibility data; the playbook needs a page-level feedback loop to work around the missing query data.

**Google — Search Profiles / Preferred Sources / Audience Loyalty features**
Analysis by Barry Adams, surfaced via **Aleyda Solís**; Preferred Sources expansion surfaced via **Lily Ray**.
→ Why it matters: Google is rewarding owned-audience and brand following — relevant to distribution strategy.

## Industry / context

**The Atlantic (Will Oremus) — on manipulating AI answers**
Surfaced via **Lily Ray** (interviewed for it). Covers self-promotional listicles, Reddit astroturfing and other manipulation tactics now under scrutiny.
→ Why it matters: signals which tactics are becoming reputational/algorithmic liabilities.

**Suganthan Mohanadasan — the "Layer 2" agent-readiness protocols**
Surfaced via **Lily Ray**. Walks through llms.txt, Markdown-for-agents, MCP server cards, A2A agent cards, Web Bot Auth, etc., and whether they're worth implementing yet.
→ Why it matters: the emerging technical layer for making sites usable by AI agents (mostly bleeding-edge, useful to track).
