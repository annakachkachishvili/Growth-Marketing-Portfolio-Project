# Deep dive — "The science of how AI picks its sources" (Kevin Indig)

- **Author:** Kevin Indig (Growth advisor; Growth Memo)

- **Published:** Feb 2026
- **Read & summarized:** 2026-06-12 — *my notes, drawn from the article and Search Engine Land's coverage of the study.*

## Why it's in this collection
It turns "how do I get cited by AI?" from opinion into **data**. This is the empirical backbone for the content-structure decisions in a playbook.

## Core finding
Across an analysis of **1.2 million AI answers and 18,012 verified citations**, citations follow a consistent **"ski ramp" pattern: ~44% of citations come from the first 30% of a page's content.** The pattern held across randomized validation batches.

## Why this happens
LLMs are trained heavily on journalism and academic writing that use a **"bottom line up front"** structure, so they over-weight the opening of a document. The study also found ChatGPT favors passages with **direct definitions, a balanced (non-hype) tone, and dense entities.**

## What to do about it
- **Front-load the key insight** at both the article and paragraph level — don't bury the answer under a long narrative intro.
- Prioritize **clarity and information density** over clever first sentences.
- **Structured, briefing-style content outperforms narrative "ultimate guides"** for AI retrieval — a meaningful break from a decade of long-form-narrative SEO advice.

## How it connects to the rest of the research
- Reinforces **Steve Toth's** "declarative passages / short dependency hops" and **Jakob/Jes Scholz-style "chunking"** advice surfaced in Aleyda's roadmap.
- Complements **Aleyda Solís's** "representative prompt library" — Indig is about *what gets cited*, Aleyda about *how to measure whether you are*.
- Pairs with **Lily Ray's** quality warning: front-loading helps retrieval, but the content still has to be accurate and grounded to survive.

## Playbook takeaway
Content structure is now a citation lever, not just a readability nicety. The playbook's writing standard should require: **answer-first openings, defined terms, entity-dense early sections, and a measured tone** — then verify visibility with prompt tracking.
