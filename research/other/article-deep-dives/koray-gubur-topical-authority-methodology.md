## Why it's in this collection
Koray's framework is the deepest answer to "how should content be *structured* so search engines and LLMs understand a brand as authoritative on a topic." It underpins the "foundation/structure" layer of a playbook.

## Core concept
**Topical Authority = Topical Coverage + Historical Data.** To rank (and be cited), a site must cover *all* the semantic variations, sub-topics, and entailed queries of a niche (coverage), while building a track record of reliability and user-trust signals (historical data). The goal is to be processed as the comprehensive, expert source for an entire topic — not just a single keyword.

## Key building blocks
- **Topical map** — a structured plan of every sub-topic, entity, and query variation in a niche, organized into a **semantic content network** (core/pillar nodes + outer/supporting nodes) connected by contextual internal links.
- **Entities & semantics over keywords** — content is built around entities and their attributes/relations (predicates), aligning with how Google's NLP (BERT/MUM-style) and LLMs interpret meaning. (See his "Authority Signature" post: Google can even attribute topical authority to *authors* per-topic.)
- **Cost of Retrieval** — make it cheap/efficient for a search engine to crawl, understand, and rank your content (clean structure, fast delivery, unambiguous semantics). Lower retrieval cost = better treatment.
- **Momentum & history** — publish the network with coverage and consistency so the site accrues historical trust for the topic over time.

## How it shows up in his recent practice (from his posts in this repo)
- **Visual Semantics + Algorithmic Authorship** — a page should support one search intent from multiple angles (visual, verbal, commercial, contextual), not just text.
- **Entitization fixes** — consolidating authority diluted across multiple domains into one (he used the Knowledge Graph API to diagnose this), after which the same documents performed better.
- Applied to large **programmatic SEO** page groups, where small per-template relevance gains multiply across the whole group.

## Playbook takeaway
Before producing content at scale, build the **topical map**: define the entities, sub-topics, and query network for the SaaS category, then structure content as a connected semantic network with disciplined internal linking and a clean, low-retrieval-cost technical setup. Coverage + structure + history is what compounds — and it's the durable opposite of publishing disconnected, keyword-by-keyword AI articles.
