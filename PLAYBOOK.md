## 1. Purpose & scope

There are two different things people call "AI SEO," and most of the chaos in this space comes from mixing them:

1. **SEO done with the help of AI** — how we work: strategy, research, drafting, updating, all with AI as the tool.
2. **Being present in the AI of other people** — where we appear: when a buyer's ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Google AI Overview answers them, our brand is in the answer.

This playbook's spine is the **first** direction — producing SEO content with AI is the actual day-to-day job. The second direction enters in exactly three labeled places, because it changes the *specifications* of what we produce (Layer 1), the way citations are *earned* (Layer 3), and what we *measure* (Layer 4). One operation, two surfaces where results show up — connected, never confused.

**Who this is for:** a B2B SaaS like 100Hires — small team, comparison-driven buyers, no big budget, one operator with AI tools and judgment. Every recommendation is scoped to that situation, which is why the same evidence sometimes leads me to different conclusions than the experts who produced it. **Where this playbook's claims stop:** see the boundary conditions at the end of [Weaknesses](#8-weaknesses-of-this-playbook).

## 3. Operating principles

1. **AI search is an expansion of SEO, not a separate channel.** No separate team, no separate strategy, no separate content (source: Jake Ward, [LinkedIn post from 11.06.2026](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7470722586423824384/) · [collected copy](research/linkedin-posts/jake-ward.md)). The mechanism, not just the opinion: AI answers still retrieve through search — behind one prompt runs a fan-out of ordinary Google/Bing queries (source: Jesse Cunningham, [video from ~04.2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O9Ys-6ArGVs) · [transcript](research/youtube-transcripts/jesse-cunningham/ranking-on-google-ai-overviews-and-chatgpt-on-autopilot.md)), and Google's own AI Overviews now appear for 86.7% of prompts — 88.5% for commercial ones (source: Tomek Rudzki / Peec AI, [500K-prompt study, Apr 2026](https://peec.ai/blog/ai-overviews-is-the-most-undertracked-ai-search-500-000-prompts-show-why) ✓).
2. **"AI content isn't the problem — worse or different content is."** The editorial bar doesn't move; AI substitutes the tool, not the standard (source: Ryan Law, [LinkedIn post from 11.06.2026](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7470769358055825408/) · [collected copy](research/linkedin-posts/ryan-law.md)). Google's measured behavior agrees: Ahrefs found a ≈zero correlation (0.011) between AI-content use and ranking position (source: Ryan Law, [article from 05.08.2025](https://ahrefs.com/blog/my-complete-ai-content-process-for-ahrefs/) · [notes](research/other/articles/ryan-law/my-complete-ai-content-process.md)).
3. **Protect classic SEO first.** "The worst thing you can do for your AI search visibility is destroy your SEO" — sites that fall in Google fall in AI shortly after (source: Lily Ray, [LinkedIn post from 02.06.2026](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7467554630458093568/) · [collected copy](research/linkedin-posts/lily-ray.md)).
4. **Prefer tactics that survive cleanups.** Loopholes work until platforms catch the pattern, then become liabilities — Google is explicitly targeting "inauthentic mentions" (source: Lily Ray, same post, 02.06.2026). Before any tactic: *is this real value, or a temporary gap in the system?*
5. **Volume is an output, not an input.** We don't pick a page count; we pick two standards — information gain and verifiability — and the right volume is whatever passes both. (My call; argued in [Section 5](#5-where-experts-disagree).)
6. **Every claim gets grounded, every number gets traced.** "LLMs are very convincing bloviators… mandating specific data sources is key" (source: Ryan Law, [article from 28.04.2026](https://ahrefs.com/blog/how-i-do-content-engineering-with-claude-code/) · [notes](research/other/articles/ryan-law/content-engineering-with-claude-code.md)). The reason is measurable: AI systems repeat repetition as truth — a fabricated detail entered AI Overviews within 24 hours in a controlled experiment (source: Lily Ray, [The AI Slop Loop, Apr 2026](https://lilyraynyc.substack.com/p/the-ai-slop-loop) · [notes](research/other/articles/lily-ray/the-ai-slop-loop.md)).
7. **Citations are the floor, not the game.** Being mentioned is not the same as being represented correctly on the questions that close deals (source: Steve Toth, [LinkedIn post from 05.06.2026](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7468647893676232704/) · [collected copy](research/linkedin-posts/steve-toth.md)). Hence the twin KPIs in Layer 4: presence *and* accuracy.

## 4. The SOP — four layers

The operation runs as four layers. Layers 1–2 are direction one (producing with AI); Layers 3–4 carry direction two (being present in the AI of other people). Each layer states its moves, cadence, and quality gate.

### Layer 1 — Foundation: map, intent, and the Citation Spec

**Goal:** structure decided before production starts, so pages accumulate into a network instead of piling up as bets.

**Moves:**
1. **Draw the intent-cluster map for the category (week one).** Read the live SERPs to learn how Google classifies each query's intent, then plan one strong page per *intent*, not per keyword — ten phrasings of "how much does an ATS cost" are one pricing page (source: Jesse Cunningham, [video from ~04.2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XxTY7LFEliQ) · [transcript](research/youtube-transcripts/jesse-cunningham/using-chatgpt-super-mode-with-api-grounded-data.md)). This is a topical map in light form; graduate to the full semantic-network methodology — entity coverage, contextual linking discipline — when pages start competing with each other in GSC (source: Koray Tuğberk Gübür, [Topical Authority guide](https://www.holisticseo.digital/theoretical-seo/topical-authority/) · [notes](research/other/articles/koray-gubur/topical-authority-methodology.md)). My non-negotiable at any depth, distilled from Gübür's methodology: one topic = one connected cluster, never orphan pages.
2. **Write every page to the Citation Spec.** The machine now reads before the human does, and it reads differently — ~44% of AI citations come from the first 30% of a page (source: Kevin Indig, [How AI picks its sources, Feb 2026](https://www.growth-memo.com/p/the-science-of-how-ai-picks-its-sources) · [notes](research/other/articles/kevin-indig/how-ai-picks-its-sources.md)). So: the answer in the first paragraph and at the top of every section; each section a self-contained, liftable chunk — one question, complete answer (source: Aleyda Solís, [AI Search roadmap / checklist, upd. May 2026](https://learningaisearch.com/) · [notes](research/other/articles/aleyda-solis/ai-search-optimization-roadmap-and-checklist.md)); declarative sentences, short dependency hops, one idea per paragraph (source: Steve Toth, [post from 05.06.2026](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7468647893676232704/)); plain definitions and a measured tone — hype reads as noise to the model (Indig, same study); real updates on a real cadence, never date-swapping — Google's own helpful-content checklist calls out faked freshness (source: Steve Toth, [post from 08.06.2026](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7469735028202201088/) + [Google's guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)).
3. **Entity and author hygiene.** Consistent brand naming everywhere; Organization + `sameAs` schema; complete G2/Capterra/Crunchbase listings (source: Steve Toth, [post from 09.06.2026](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7470097463970844672/) · [collected copy](research/linkedin-posts/steve-toth.md)). Give content a real, consistent, named author with a proper page and Person schema — Google designed a mechanism ("Authority Signature," from the *Determining Topic Authority* patent) to attribute topical authority to authors per-topic (source: Koray Gübür, [post from 08.06.2026](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7469849545301102593/) · [collected copy](research/linkedin-posts/koray-tugberk-gubur.md)). Honest caveat: a patent proves Google *built* the mechanism, not that it is fully switched on — the recommendation costs little and is worded accordingly.

4. **The pre-publish reader test (my practice, not found in my sources).** Before writing a page, write the 2–3 sentences we *want* an AI to say about 100Hires on that topic — the target quote — and build the key chunk around it. Then test with the reader itself: paste the chunk alone into a model and ask "what would you tell a buyer based on this?" If it re-tells us wrong or flat, the chunk failed *before* publishing, not after. The machine that writes with us simulates the machine that will read us. (Adjacent practices in my sources: Toth's checklist-grading GPT, [post from 08.06.2026](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7469735028202201088/), and Breton's AI-persona beta-testing, [post from 05.05.2026](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7457444471383683072/) · [collected copy](research/linkedin-posts/gael-breton.md) — neither uses it for citation-targeting.)

**Quality gate:** no page ships without a cluster home, a named author, a pass against the Citation Spec, and a passed reader test on its key chunk.

### Layer 2 — Production: the three-legged system

AI content fails in exactly three ways — false, empty, or generic. Each leg of the production system kills one failure mode. No single expert in my sources holds all three legs, because each sells his own; the assembly is mine.

**Leg 1 — against FALSE content: ground everything.**
- Wire real data into the workflow, never model memory: keyword/SERP data via API, live SERP reads, product facts from a source-of-truth file (source: Ryan Law, [article from 28.04.2026](https://ahrefs.com/blog/how-i-do-content-engineering-with-claude-code/); the grounded custom-GPT pattern: Jesse Cunningham, [video from ~04.2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XxTY7LFEliQ) · [transcript](research/youtube-transcripts/jesse-cunningham/using-chatgpt-super-mode-with-api-grounded-data.md)).
- Ground in *customer language* too: mine sales calls and support tickets for the words buyers actually use, as seed keywords (source: Ryan Law, [post from 08.06.2026](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7469755485517271040/) · [collected copy](research/linkedin-posts/ryan-law.md)).
- Give agents business context to read *before* acting — a memory file of what the company is, sells, and sounds like — or the output is generic by construction (source: Julian Goldie, [video from ~11.06.2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YSYjs3L3MMU) · [transcript](research/youtube-transcripts/julian-goldie/how-i-run-my-entire-seo-from-one-dashboard.md) — credited to the one lesson of his I keep).
- Know the limit: grounding kills *data* hallucination, not bad analysis. In the grounded-GPT demo itself, the API failed mid-recording and the model silently switched to "estimated but realistic results" — the exact failure the tool claims to solve ([transcript](research/youtube-transcripts/jesse-cunningham/using-chatgpt-super-mode-with-api-grounded-data.md), and the reason human verification stays in the loop).

**Leg 2 — against EMPTY content: require information gain.**
- Research before writing: find what the existing top results *fail* to answer (the "Frustrated Reader" method — a reader-roleplay pass over what ranks, then research into Reddit/YouTube/forums where people share what they actually learned), and only write when there's something worth saying (source: Gael Breton, [LinkedIn post from 21.04.2026](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7452295174195507200/) · [collected copy](research/linkedin-posts/gael-breton.md)).
- The penalty for skipping this leg is documented: sites that scaled zero-gain templates sit at 5-year visibility lows after the 2026 updates (source: Lily Ray, [post from 08.06.2026](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7469712876962807808/) · [collected copy](research/linkedin-posts/lily-ray.md)).

**Leg 3 — against GENERIC content: encode the criteria, keep a human on top.**
- Document the editorial process as versioned skill files the model follows step by step, with each step's output saved separately so failures can be located and fixed at the stage that caused them (source: Ryan Law, [article from 28.04.2026](https://ahrefs.com/blog/how-i-do-content-engineering-with-claude-code/) · [notes](research/other/articles/ryan-law/content-engineering-with-claude-code.md)).
- Front-load small human direction — a one-paragraph angle at the start beats heavy editing at the end (Law, same article). "90% of success comes from good topic selection… only use AI on topics where you can tell if the article is actually good" (source: Ryan Law, [article from 05.08.2025](https://ahrefs.com/blog/my-complete-ai-content-process-for-ahrefs/)).
- QA against Google's own 32-question helpful-content self-assessment, graded by a custom GPT so scoring is consistent (source: Steve Toth, [post from 08.06.2026](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7469735028202201088/)).
- Capture refinements as reusable SOPs before the context window runs out — "the deck was the deliverable, but the SOP is the asset" (source: Steve Toth, [post from 10.06.2026](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7470459834891395073/) · [collected copy](research/linkedin-posts/steve-toth.md)).

**Volume rule (from [Section 5](#5-where-experts-disagree)):** editorial pieces start at ~2/week and adjust to capacity; comparison/alternatives pages are built programmatically only where real data exists — one page per true competitor or segment, dozens bounded by entity reality, never thousands; mass AI articles never, because they fail the verification bound on day one.

**Quality gate:** a draft ships only when all three legs passed — data traced, gain named, criteria met — and a human signed the judgment.

### Layer 3 — Distribution & consensus: how AI recommendations are earned

**Goal:** be the answer AI gives buyers — through voices AI trusts.

**The core fact:** for branded queries, only 23% of AI citations go to content the brand owns; ~77% goes to third-party sources — 48% earned media, 30% commercial content (source: Omniscient Digital, [study of 23,387 sources across 240 branded queries, 08.01.2026](https://beomniscient.com/blog/how-llms-source-brand-information/) ✓). And for commercial "best X" queries specifically, ~43.8% of ChatGPT citations are blog-style listicles, ~79% of them recently updated (source: Glen Allsopp / Ahrefs, [study of 26,283 source URLs across 750 "best X" queries](https://ahrefs.com/blog/best-lists-research/) ✓ — popularized without attribution and with widened scope in Jake Ward's [post from 09.06.2026](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7470081516916776960/); the tracing story is in Section 5).

**Moves, in priority order (the trust hierarchy — argued in [Section 5](#5-where-experts-disagree)):**
1. **Independent trusted third-party first.** Run the prompt-audit reconnaissance: enter the prompts we want to appear in, export which sources the AI actually cites, find where we're missing, and get added *legitimately* — real reviews, complete listings, genuine comparison-page inclusion (source: Jake Ward, [post from 11.06.2026](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7470722586423824384/) · [collected copy](research/linkedin-posts/jake-ward.md)). Prioritize by the AI's revealed trust — the sources that keep reappearing in our category's answers — and by listing authority (source: Steve Toth, [post from 09.06.2026](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7470097463970844672/)). Scope note from the Allsopp study: 35% of cited lists sit on low-authority domains — relevance plus revealed trust is the bar, not raw domain size.
2. **Our own comparison/alternatives pages.** Best-of and alternatives pages filled with real data, kept genuinely fresh (source: Jake Ward, [post from 09.06.2026](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7470081516916776960/) · [collected copy](research/linkedin-posts/jake-ward.md); the freshness bar comes from the underlying study — ~79% of cited lists were recently updated, [Allsopp/Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/best-lists-research/) ✓) — bounded by entity reality per the volume rule.
3. **Authentic first-party multi-format, openly.** Repurpose real content under our own name across formats and surfaces; across 75,000 brands, YouTube mentions correlate more strongly with AI visibility than any other channel measured (source: Ahrefs, [75K-brand report, May 2026](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260526119691/en/Across-75000-Brands-YouTube-Mentions-Are-the-Strongest-Signal-of-AI-Visibility-New-Ahrefs-Report-Reveals) ✓ — correlational: brands big on YouTube tend to be big everywhere). Bounded by what we can maintain well, not by what a tool can generate.
4. **Paid placements only as declared advertising** (my rule). Much "independent" presence runs on sponsorship or affiliate commissions; AI may cite it, but a bought mention is reach, not proof — it never counts as earned consensus in our measurement.
5. **Disguise never.** No fake "news coverage," no self-promotional lists dressed as independent, no astroturfed Reddit — the exact class Google is penalizing (source: Lily Ray, [posts from 02.06 and 08.06.2026](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7467554630458093568/) · [collected copy](research/linkedin-posts/lily-ray.md)). **My test for the line: would the tactic still work if the AI's user could see who published it — and who paid for it?** If it only works while the reader doesn't know, it's manufactured consensus, and it dies in the next cleanup.

**Quality gate:** every consensus move passes the disguise test; every placement is classified (earned / paid-declared / owned) before it's counted.

### Layer 4 — Measurement: polling, not rank tracking

**Goal:** know whether we're present, whether we're represented *correctly*, and whether it's turning into business — without drowning a small team in dashboards.

**The KPI portfolio (weights shift quarterly — measure the transition, don't pick an era):**

| # | Family | What & why |
|---|--------|------------|
| 1 | **Business** | Signups/demos/pipeline — the true north; never sessions |
| 2 | **Presence** | Mentions/citations per AI platform, from the prompt audit + the GSC Generative-AI report |
| 3 | **Accuracy** | % of deal-breaker prompts answered *correctly* about us — pricing, integrations, compliance (my Presence×Accuracy call — see below) |
| 4 | **Classic** | Rankings + organic traffic — still reported, because Google currently sends ~190× more traffic than ChatGPT (source: Ahrefs, [research, 2026](https://ahrefs.com/blog/chatgpt-has-12-percent-of-googles-search-volume/) ✓ — with the footnote that referrer data undercounts AI-sourced visits, so the true gap is smaller; even divided by ten, the point stands) |
| 5 | **Owned audience** | Newsletter/social growth — fewer than ⅓ of Google searches now send a click (source: Rand Fishkin / SparkToro–Similarweb, [2026 study](https://sparktoro.com/blog/in-2026-less-than-one-third-of-google-searches-still-send-a-click/) ✓ — clickstream-panel estimate; direction widely accepted, exact fraction soft), and Google itself is building audience-loyalty surfaces (source: Barry Adams via Aleyda Solís, [post from 10.06.2026](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7470485508435886080/) · [collected copy](research/linkedin-posts/aleyda-solis.md)) |

**Moves:**
1. **The monthly deal-breaker audit (start week one).** The ~15 questions an ATS buyer actually asks — pricing, integrations, SSO, compliance — across ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Copilot; log who gets credit and *what is said*. Run every prompt 3× and record "correct in k of 3," never a single yes/no — one run of a prompt is noise (source: Kevin Indig, [post from 08.06.2026](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7469704803720482816/) · [collected copy](research/linkedin-posts/kevin-indig.md); audit design: Steve Toth, [post from 09.06.2026](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7470097463970844672/)). Store raw answers from day one — free now, a baseline later.
2. **Build the prompt set representatively** — products, audiences, journey stages, competitors, constraints; not a tool's default list (source: Aleyda Solís, [post from 08.06.2026](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7469723521947131905/) · [collected copy](research/linkedin-posts/aleyda-solis.md)). Convert real-search-volume keywords into natural AI prompts at scale with Ray's spreadsheet method (source: Lily Ray, [post from 09.06.2026](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7470166067043147776/) · [collected copy](research/linkedin-posts/lily-ray.md)).
3. **The Presence×Accuracy loop.** Presence without accuracy is a trap: AI can mention us and mis-describe us, and a wrong promise converts into churn, not revenue. So accuracy is its own KPI — and the *repair channel* is the same third-party network that amplifies us: when the audit finds AI saying something wrong, fix the sources it reads (listings, comparison pages, entity data), then re-audit (mechanism: Steve Toth, [post from 09.06.2026](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7470097463970844672/); attribute-level tracking: Kevin Indig, [post from 09.06.2026](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7470197035565002752/)).
4. **The page-level feedback loop.** The new GSC Generative-AI report gives AI-feature impressions but no queries and no clicks (source: Koray Gübür, [post from 03.06.2026](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7467874512357797888/) · [collected copy](research/linkedin-posts/koray-tugberk-gubur.md)) — so learn at page level: change a page to spec, watch its AI impressions move, conclude, roll out. Pairs of similar pages on different spec versions work as natural experiments.
5. **The Spec Loop (monthly).** The Citation Spec lives as a versioned file in the repo — the same pattern as documenting editorial process in versioned skill files (source: Ryan Law, [article from 28.04.2026](https://ahrefs.com/blog/how-i-do-content-engineering-with-claude-code/)) and capturing refinements as reusable SOPs before they evaporate (source: Steve Toth, [post from 10.06.2026](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7470459834891395073/) · [collected copy](research/linkedin-posts/steve-toth.md)). Each month: compare cited pages against ignored ones, identify which spec elements the winners had, commit spec v+1, retrofit the top pages. Content is the deliverable; **the loop is the asset** — a spec that has learned our niche for months is the one thing competitors can't copy.
6. **Graduate rigor when stakes demand it.** The full designed prompt panel — per-platform samples, personas, confidence intervals (source: Kevin Indig, [post from 09.06.2026](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7470197035565002752/)) — becomes worth its 2–3 hrs/week the moment a decision depends on a small difference. Until then, k-of-3 tracking is honest; half-done rigor produces confident wrong numbers. Rigor follows stakes, not the calendar (argued in [Section 5](#5-where-experts-disagree)).

**Quality gate:** the monthly report to Alex is one small table — five KPI families, movement arrows — not a 40-metric export. If reporting starts eating production time, owned-audience and classic drop to quarterly; business and accuracy never drop.

## 5. Where experts disagree

*A note on method: I frame-checked every disagreement before taking a side, and most of the famous fights in this space turned out to be mis-drawn — sides that weren't really opposed, titles mistaken for methods, facts mistaken for recommendations. Before taking sides, check whether the sides are real. Three disagreements survived the check; a fourth dissolved under it, and I kept the dissolution because it's the more useful result.*

### 5.1 Scale vs. information gain — a fight I think is mis-framed

**What Author A recommends:** scale. Jake Ward's signature results are programmatic — "13,000+ pages in 3 hours," and the 1,800-article "SEO heist" (source: Jake Ward's own case studies via [jakeward.io](https://jakeward.io); the heist aftermath documented by [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/ai-powered-seo-heist-copying-website-loses-significant-google-traffic/)). Julian Goldie's variant: one keyword → 5 auto-deployed articles across 5 sites (source: Julian Goldie, [video from ~10.06.2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8NAjD6HcHzk) · [transcript](research/youtube-transcripts/julian-goldie/how-to-rank-number-1-with-claude-fable-5-ai-seo.md) ✖ for its claims).

**What Author B recommends:** restraint. Ryan Law runs the best automation system in my corpus at a company with more content capacity than almost anyone — and explicitly refuses to scale: "we have no plans to 'scale content'" (source: Ryan Law, [article from 28.04.2026](https://ahrefs.com/blog/how-i-do-content-engineering-with-claude-code/)). Gael Breton: the bottleneck isn't writing, it's having something worth writing (source: Gael Breton, [post from 21.04.2026](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7452295174195507200/)). Lily Ray: template-scaled sites sit at 5-year visibility lows (source: Lily Ray, [post from 08.06.2026](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7469712876962807808/)).

**Which side I take and why:** operationally, the restraint side — with one bounded exception — but I reject the framing first. "Scale vs. quality" flattens three different practices into one side — programmatic pages built from real structured data, mass AI articles, and templated listicle spam are not the same thing — and quantity and information gain are two separate axes: an empty article is slop at any volume, and 5,000 pages can each be true. The real question is narrower: **at what volume does output outrun your ability to verify it?** My rule for 100Hires: *volume is an output, not an input* — two standards (information gain required, verification required), and whatever volume passes both is the right volume. In practice: editorial at ~2/week; programmatic comparison pages bounded by entity reality (dozens, each filled with real data — justified because ~44% of ChatGPT citations for best-X-class queries are exactly such lists, per the verified Allsopp/Ahrefs study); mass AI articles never. The risk backdrop makes the bounds strict: when the person with the most capacity to scale chooses not to, that's information — and the best-documented scale experiment (the heist) ended at ~190K visits by Dec 2023, *below* its ~200K pre-campaign baseline (source: [The Decoder's analysis](https://the-decoder.com/ai-powered-seo-heist-copying-website-loses-significant-google-traffic/)). And when structure is added — topical maps, intent clusters, internal linking — bounded quantity stops being risk and becomes a network: **quantity × quality × structure = authority; any factor at zero makes the product zero.**

### 5.2 Is AI-search visibility a new discipline, or just SEO?

**What Author A recommends:** it's SEO. "Please STOP doing 'AEO'… treat AI search as an expansion of SEO" (source: Jake Ward, [post from 11.06.2026](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7470722586423824384/)); "Getting cited in AI Overviews IS just SEO… but citations are the floor, not the game" (source: Steve Toth, [post from 05.06.2026](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7468647893676232704/)).

**What Author B recommends:** it's a new practice. Kevin Indig builds AI-visibility measurement unlike anything in classic SEO — designed panels, personas, confidence intervals (source: Kevin Indig, [posts from 07–09.06.2026](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7470197035565002752/) · [collected copy](research/linkedin-posts/kevin-indig.md)); Aleyda Solís maintains a whole separate optimization roadmap ([LearningAISearch](https://learningaisearch.com/)).

**Which side I take and why:** mostly SEO — but I got there by checking incentives first. "New discipline" sells tools, courses, and retainers; "just SEO" sells nothing extra — though the just-SEO camp has incentives too (established experts defending the centrality of their skillset). Incentives roughly cancel, so the verdict rests on mechanism: **you cannot be visible in a machine that retrieves from an index you don't rank in.** AI search runs *on* search — the fan-out behind every prompt, AI Overviews inside Google firing on 86.7% of prompts (✓), sites falling in Google falling in AI right after. What I concede to the "new" camp is real: every page now has a **second reader** — the machine reads first and decides whether the human ever sees us, and it reads differently (first 30%, liftable chunks, declarative sentences, entities). Writing one page for two readers at once is a genuinely new craft, and measurement genuinely is polling now. So: the work is SEO on the same infrastructure; what's new is the output specs, the measurement — and a *literacy*, not a department. The same models write with us in production and read us in retrieval; one skill applied at both ends. The org-chart receipt: 100Hires isn't hiring an AEO team; it's hiring one AI-literate marketer.

### 5.3 Whose voice does the AI trust: self-made omnipresence, or third-party consensus?

**What Author A recommends:** manufacture ubiquity yourself. Julian Goldie: AI recommends whoever appears across the most sources and formats — so turn one topic into ~8 self-published formats pushed across platforms, including "news articles that read like actual coverage" (source: Julian Goldie, [sponsored Ampcast video from ~06.2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nTyLa_zavfs) · [transcript](research/youtube-transcripts/julian-goldie/get-chatgpt-to-recommend-any-product-over-competitors.md) ✖ vendor numbers).

**What Author B recommends:** earn it from third parties. The verified data: only 23% of branded-query citations go to owned content (Omniscient ✓); best-X lists dominate commercial queries (Allsopp/Ahrefs ✓); fix the entity graph and the highest-authority listings first (source: Steve Toth, [post from 09.06.2026](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7470097463970844672/)). And the constraint: manipulated, inauthentic signal is what platforms are penalizing (source: Lily Ray, [post from 02.06.2026](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7467554630458093568/)).

**Which side I take and why:** I side with the earn camp on where the money and effort go — but as a hierarchy rather than an absolute, because Goldie's core mechanism can't be dismissed (across 75K brands, YouTube mentions — first-party, multi-format — correlate most with AI visibility ✓, though correlation isn't causation), and "third-party" itself splits in two: **paid third-party is only half third-party** (many "independent" lists run on sponsorship or affiliate commissions — a bought mention is reach, not proof), and **third-party authority does the heavy lifting** — the signal is "a source the AI already trusts mentioned us," not "someone mentioned us." So my hierarchy, as implemented in Layer 3: independent trusted third-party first; our own comparison pages second; open authentic multi-format third; declared paid placements as advertising only; disguise never. The line is a test I can apply to any future tactic: *would it still work if the AI's user could see who published it — and who paid for it?*

### 5.4 (Sidebar) A disagreement I checked and found false

"Deep semantic methodology vs. pragmatic speed" — Koray's full topical-map method against Jesse's read-the-SERP-and-ship — dissolved on inspection: the claims live on different layers (architecture vs. targeting vs. tooling), and Jesse's intent clusters *are* a topical map in light form; my own research synthesis had already grouped them as allies. The only real tension is how much structure before the first page ships, and that's a dial, not a side: intent-clusters as the one-week map-lite on day one, graduating toward the full methodology when pages start competing with each other (the practical signal: query cannibalization in GSC). The doctrine that fell out — **rigor is a dial you turn as returns justify it, not a religion you join on day one** — resolves the measurement question the same way, which is usually the sign a principle is real. I kept this dissolved disagreement in the playbook because "the sides aren't real" is sometimes the strongest judgment available.

## 6. What I rejected and why

*Four rejections, four different kinds of "no" — evidence, incentive, dependency, context. The spread is deliberate: I reject ideas from my lowest-trust source and from one of my most trusted ones, because rejection logic that only fires at salesmen isn't judgment, it's a mood. Each rejection starts with the strongest honest version of the idea.*

### R1 — Scaled AI page generation as a growth strategy *(rejected on evidence)*
The idea, steelmanned: marginal cost near zero; programmatic SEO is legitimate where a large *true* entity space exists; and it demonstrably produced traffic — Jake Ward's "SEO heist" really did reach 3.6M visits (source: his own public claims; aftermath via [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/ai-powered-seo-heist-copying-website-loses-significant-google-traffic/)).
Rejected for 100Hires on four independent grounds: **(1) entity reality** — the ATS category holds a few hundred true pages; page #501 is fiction with a template. **(2) the verification bound** — a 1–2 person team cannot fact-check thousands of pages, and unverifiable output is what Layer 2 exists to prevent. **(3) the documented outcome** — the best-known case ended *below its pre-campaign baseline* (~610K → 190K visits vs. ~200K, by Dec 2023), and the same template pattern was penalized domain-wide in 2026 (source: Lily Ray, [post from 08.06.2026](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7469712876962807808/)). **(4) asymmetric stakes** — one domain, a trust-selling product, HR buyers. The incentive tell: the loudest advocates sold the shovel (Ward co-founded Byword, the tool that writes the articles) — and even he now preaches earned consensus (source: [post from 11.06.2026](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7470722586423824384/)). What survives: the bounded comparison-page set in Layer 3 — the honest 10% of this idea.

### R2 — Rebuilding the pipeline around each newest model *(rejected on incentive shape)*
The idea, steelmanned: staying current with model capability is real leverage (source: Julian Goldie, ["How to Rank #1 with (the newest model)," video from ~10.06.2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8NAjD6HcHzk) · [transcript](research/youtube-transcripts/julian-goldie/how-to-rank-number-1-with-claude-fable-5-ai-seo.md)).
Rejected because the strategy is **designed to expire**: the pitch is framed around a model available "for two weeks" — the novelty is load-bearing, and adopting it means perpetual rebuilding: new model → new hack → new pipeline, forever. A strategy whose core input rotates monthly cannot accumulate anything, and this playbook runs on compounding (the Spec Loop). Evidence quality seals it: "ranking in 24 hours" claimed on his own funnel sites, unverifiable (✖); the sibling video is sponsored with vendor-supplied numbers (✖). The durable version already exists and Layer 2 uses it: a model-agnostic pipeline where process is the asset and models are swappable parts (source: Ryan Law, [article from 28.04.2026](https://ahrefs.com/blog/how-i-do-content-engineering-with-claude-code/)). I'd rather build the thing that survives the next model release than the thing that celebrates it — and credit stays where due: Goldie's memory-layer-first lesson is kept in Layer 2, because rejecting a person's worst idea doesn't require rejecting his best.

### R3 — Tool-locked stacks sold as the method itself *(rejected on dependency)*
The idea, steelmanned: grounding is correct (Layer 2, leg 1), and the tools are cheap and fast to start with (source: Jesse Cunningham's DataForSEO custom GPT — "a senior SEO consultant in a box," [video from ~04.2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XxTY7LFEliQ); Ampcast as "the system," [sponsored video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nTyLa_zavfs)).
Rejected because the sellers market an *implementation* as a *capability*: **(1) demonstrated fragility, on camera** — mid-demo the API credit lapsed and the "consultant in a box" *silently* switched to "estimated but realistic results"; the failure mode the tool claims to kill returned without warning ([transcript](research/youtube-transcripts/jesse-cunningham/using-chatgpt-super-mode-with-api-grounded-data.md)). **(2) dependency economics** — rented capability inside someone else's sales funnel; pricing, terms, and existence outside our control (Ampcast was literally pre-launch in the video). **(3) skill formation** — a junior who learns the tool learns buttons; one who learns the principle can rebuild the stack in any tool, which is the "figure things out" capability 100Hires actually hires for. The replacement rule: principles live in the spec; tools pass through — any implementation must be swappable in a weekend.

### R4 — The publisher-defense posture: blocking AI bots *(rejected on context)*
The idea, steelmanned — and it's strong: for publishers, bot defense and licensing leverage are rational; LLMs return under one-third of one percent of traffic share to News/Media while consuming their product (source: Shahzad Abbas / Define Media Group, relayed by Aleyda Solís, [post from 11.06.2026](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7470746984648212480/) · [collected copy](research/linkedin-posts/aleyda-solis.md)).
Rejected as **right advice for the wrong patient**: a publisher monetizes attention on its own pages, so being read by AI without a click is pure loss — but a B2B SaaS monetizes *being chosen*, so being read and cited by AI is free distribution at the exact moment of buyer deliberation. Same mechanism, opposite P&L sign; blocking the bots would sever the citation channel all of Layer 3 is built to win. Worth stating plainly: this is not a trust downgrade of the source — Aleyda is among my most trusted curators and her relay is accurate. The flaw isn't truth; it's *transfer*. Advice inherits the context of its author, and context must be re-checked at the border: "who said it" can never substitute for "who is it for."

## 7. My original ideas

### Idea 1 — Trace-before-trust: verification as a shipped practice, not a value
Everyone in my sources says "fact-check AI output." Nobody shows a workflow for it. While building this playbook I ended up practicing one, so I'm proposing the thing I already did: **every load-bearing number in our content gets a chain of custody** — where I found it, the primary source, its scope conditions, and a status (verified / pending / unverifiable) — kept in a ledger that ships internally with the piece and gets re-checked quarterly.

I know it works because it already caught things. The most-quoted statistic in my research (43.8%) turned out to be real but borrowed uncredited and quietly widened in scope — I found the actual study and the true condition (the full story is in Sections 5 and 10). A second number (the "57%" my own notes carried for the Omniscient study) was corrected the same way. I decoded exact timestamps for all 31 collected posts from their URL IDs. The ledger even cut one of my own ideas from this very section when tracing showed its central claim was too wide. None of my ten experts has anything like this.

Why it could work for 100Hires: accuracy is literally the fear in their own hiring text ("good vs. AI-generated garbage"); the information supply chain is contaminated and getting worse — a fabricated detail entered AI Overviews within 24 hours in Ray's experiment, and AI answers are frequently correct-but-ungrounded (sources: Lily Ray, [The AI Slop Loop, Apr 2026](https://lilyraynyc.substack.com/p/the-ai-slop-loop); NYT accuracy report via [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-ai-overviews-accuracy-wrong-answers-analysis-473837) ✓); and verified content is precisely what survives cleanups. There's also a public form: verification-as-content — *"we traced five famous AI-SEO statistics to their primary sources; here's what they actually say."* Original research is what earns citations, and it costs two days, because the process is already built. This idea isn't a proposal; it's a demonstration — the receipts are in this repository.

### Idea 2 — The B2C2B pull, transferred from my own practice: let the end-user's voice sell to the buyer
In my beauty-clinic marketing work I ran a B2C2B funnel: we built interest and demand among *patients* for professional injectable and cosmetic lines — products only doctors could buy — so patients started asking their doctors for them, and the doctors' orders followed. Demand was created one level below the buyer, then pulled through.

The same physics now applies to an ATS, and AI search is what makes it work: **AI answers collapse the wall between audience information spaces.** When an HR buyer asks "best ATS for startups," the fan-out retrieves from everything — including candidate forums, threads about application experiences, "which companies actually reply" discussions. The fan-out doesn't check who a thread was written for; end-user sentiment becomes buyer-facing evidence automatically. Candidates can't name an ATS to their employer the way a patient names a cream — but they don't need to: they *see* the ATS at the application touchpoint, they talk about the experience publicly, and that talk is exactly the third-party consensus layer this playbook's Layer 3 tries to win. The pull happens inside the retrieval, not inside a conversation.

The honest version of the claim: career-tools companies (Jobscan and others) own candidate content as a *direct B2C business* — adjacent, not competing. What none of them, and none of my ten sources, describe is candidate content run as a *deliberate pull strategy for the employer-side purchase*. The honest risks: candidate discussion of ATS'es skews negative ("the ATS rejected me") — which is also the opening, because the one ATS candidates don't hate becomes differentiated; and this pull is slower and softer than my cosmetics case was.

The $0 test, two weeks: add candidate-sentiment prompts to the monthly audit ("which ATS is least painful to apply through?") to baseline what AI currently says about applying through 100Hires vs. competitors; publish one touchpoint page written for candidates (what actually happens to your application inside 100Hires); watch candidate-community mentions and whether the sentiment queries shift. If it moves, we've opened a consensus channel competitors aren't watching; if it doesn't, we've spent two weeks and one page.

## 8. Weaknesses of this playbook

- Every expert in my sources is selling something — courses, tools, newsletters, agencies. All ten. So the numbers I cite are marketing numbers unless I could trace them to a source (for some I could — that's what the verification ledger is for; where I couldn't, I say so in the text). The honest limit: this playbook is built on evidence of persuasion quality, not laboratory quality.
- I haven't run these tactics myself. This is research-backed, not experience-backed. After 90 days of real execution some parts of this will look naive — I just can't tell you today which ones.
- It ages fast. My material is from May–June 2026, and this field changes monthly (the GSC AI report launched while I was collecting). That's why the playbook carries its own re-verification cadence — without it, parts of this are stale by autumn.
- My experts are the loud ones. I picked people who post publicly and often, which over-samples personal brands with something to sell — and misses the quiet in-house operators who actually do this job at companies like 100Hires. The people most similar to my future colleagues are the least represented in my sources. And failures are invisible: nobody posts "my AI content strategy quietly died."
- Ten experts is ten experts. A different, equally defensible ten would have produced a somewhat different playbook. I chose carefully (the methodology is in [research/methodology.md](research/methodology.md)), but I don't pretend my sample is the whole field.
- Size transfer isn't guaranteed. Most of my proof comes from big US/UK operators or established domains like Ahrefs. Nobody in my sources runs content at 100Hires' exact size and authority level, so some recommendations may need scaling down in ways I've flagged but can't fully predict.
**Specific things that might not work — with exits named in advance:**
- The comparison-page exception (Layer 3) leans on best-X citation data. If our comparison pages earn no citations in the prompt audit after 60 days, the exception shrinks and this becomes an editorial-only strategy — I name that exit now rather than defend the plan later.
- The B2C2B pull (Idea 2) may be too slow or too soft for an ATS — the two-week test decides, and its failure costs one page.
- The one-week map-lite may underestimate how much structure the category needs; the tell is query cannibalization appearing in GSC, and the response is graduating to the full topical map earlier.
- Five KPI families may be two too many for a 1–2 person operation; the pre-committed cut order: owned-audience and classic drop to quarterly, business and accuracy never.
- The deepest assumption of all: that AI answers keep retrieving *through* search indexes. If the chatbots decouple — their own indexes, direct licensing — the "expansion of SEO" premise weakens and this playbook needs a structural revision, not a refresh. Today the fan-out is observable; this is a watch-item, and it's on the quarterly review.

- And the honest boundary, from lived experience: this playbook assumes English-market infrastructure — review platforms to earn consensus on, listicle ecosystems to get cited from, entity graphs to fix, keyword APIs to ground models in. I've worked in the Georgian market, in Georgian, and most of that simply doesn't exist there: consensus lives in Facebook groups, absence (not misattribution) is the default entity state, the models are weaker in-language, and the data infrastructure barely covers the language — small-market SEO runs on judgment where big-market SEO runs on data. The flip side I've seen: over a tiny index, one genuinely good piece can dominate AI answers — in English you fight for a citation; in a small language you can *be* the index. For today this marks where the playbook's claims stop; the day 100Hires looks at a non-English market, it becomes a head start none of my sources can offer. My evidence here isn't research; it's residence.
